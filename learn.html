How encryption protects you

A plain-language guide to the cryptography behind CipherX, and what it does and does not protect.

The basics

Four ideas that explain everything else.

🔒 Encryption

Encryption scrambles readable text (plaintext) into unreadable data (ciphertext) using a secret key. Without the key, the ciphertext is useless.

Plaintext: Hello World
Ciphertext: kP3x9Zq+Rm4vT1yB7nWc…

🔑 Decryption

Decryption reverses the process. The right key turns the ciphertext back into the original message. A wrong key, or any change to the data, makes it fail.

🛡️ AES-256-GCM

The standard used to encrypt message content. It hides the data and also detects tampering, so an altered message is rejected instead of being read.

🗝️ Public and private keys

Every account has a key pair. Anyone can use your public key to lock a message for you. Only your private key can unlock it, and it never leaves your device unprotected.

How account messaging works

From sign-up to reading a message.

  1. Sign up. Your browser creates a key pair and locks the private key with your password.
  2. Share your ID. Give people your CipherX ID, like CX-7K2M9PQA.
  3. Send. The message is encrypted on your device with a fresh random key, and that key is locked for the recipient.
  4. Store. The server keeps only ciphertext. It has no key to read it.
  5. Read. The recipient's browser unlocks the key, then the message.

Being transparent: the server can see which accounts exist and when a message is sent from one ID to another. It can never see what the message says.

Good security habits

  • Use long passphrases: 12+ characters with words, numbers and symbols.
  • Send the Message ID and the passphrase through different channels.
  • Never reuse your CipherX password on other sites.
  • Log out on shared computers and keep your browser updated.
  • Only use CipherX over HTTPS once it is hosted online.

Common questions

Can CipherX read my messages?

No. Messages are encrypted in your browser and the server stores only ciphertext. It can see that one account sent something to another ID, but never the content.

What if I forget my password?

You can reset it by email, but your encryption keys are locked with the old password. Resetting creates new keys, so messages received before the reset can no longer be read. This is how true end-to-end encryption works.

What if I lose the passphrase for a Message ID?

It cannot be recovered. The passphrase is never sent to the server, so nobody, including us, can reset it.

Is it safe on a public computer?

Avoid it where you can. If you must, log out when you finish and close the browser tab, which also clears your session.

Does end-to-end encryption protect against everything?

No. It protects messages in transit and on the server. It cannot protect you if your device has malware, if someone watches your screen, or if you share your password or passphrase.
